= Bitcoin Chain Validation
:page-parent: Sharechain
:page-nav_order: 25

P2Poolv2 pays a share only for work on the bitcoin chain that the
paying block extends, give or take one block. Work on a made-up
bitcoin block earns nothing, and work on a block that lost an orphan
race still earns rewards. Miners on a hostile fork earn on the main
chain for at most the fork's first two blocks.

Every share names the bitcoin block it builds on, the previous block
hash in its bitcoin header. This page calls that block the share's
_bitcoin prev_. With no rule on it, a miner can name a bitcoin prev
that does not exist, or one on a hostile fork, and still collect
PPLNS weight. During a fork war, the fork's miners then earn from both
chains for the same proof of work.

The design has three parts:

Payout rule:: A coinbase pays a share in its PPLNS window only if that
share's bitcoin prev sits on the paying share's bitcoin chain, or one
block off it. Every node rebuilds every coinbase, so this is a
validity rule for shares.
Carried bitcoin headers:: Share messages carry the bitcoin headers
that each receiver needs to place every share's bitcoin prev. Each
node checks these received headers without calling bitcoind RPC.
Known bitcoin prev:: A node accepts a share only after it knows the
header of the share's bitcoin prev. Until then it waits for the
headers to arrive.

Fork choice and the job builder stay as they are.

In the diagram, the paying share builds on Block 102, so the paying
chain ends there. Sibling 102 and Hostile 102 both build on Block 101,
so each sits one block off the paying chain, and shares on them earn.
Hostile 103 sits two blocks off, so Share 5 earns nothing. Nodes
cannot tell a hostile fork's first block from a benign sibling. The
rule pays both, and stops paying at the fork's second block.

== Terms

Known header:: A bitcoin header in the node's header store, reported
by the local bitcoind or carried with a share and checked.
Paying chain:: For share S, the bitcoin chain ending at S's bitcoin
prev. S's coinbase extends this chain if S meets bitcoin difficulty.
Eligible:: A bitcoin block is eligible for S when the block, or its
parent, is on S's paying chain. A share is eligible for S when its
bitcoin prev is.

== Local Tree of Bitcoin Headers

Each P2Poolv2 node keeps a store of full bitcoin headers, with their
heights and parents. Acceptance and payout checks read only this store
and do not call bitcoind RPC. Three sources maintain this store:

* ZMQ `sequence` events from the local bitcoind. A block connect adds
  the block; a disconnect leaves it in place. `sequence` numbers every
  message, so a gap shows a missed one.
* The `getchaintips` RPC, at startup and after a sequence gap or a ZMQ
  reconnect. The node walks each listed branch back with
  `getblockheader` until it reaches a header already in the store.
* Carried headers with share chain headers that pass the header check.

Payout checks reach about 14 days back (the PPLNS window), and nodes
validate shares up to `PRUNE_DEPTH` (about 28 days) deep. The store
therefore keeps about six weeks of headers: roughly 6,048.

=== Siblings at the Tip

The local bitcoind cannot tell every node about every orphan. Bitcoin
Core announces a block only after making it its tip, and it ignores an
equal-work sibling that arrives as a compact block. Some bitcoinds
therefore never learn of an orphan.

A node that cannot place a share on that orphan rebuilds a different
coinbase from other nodes. It marks the paying share invalid, but the
others accept it, and the sharechain splits. Every node therefore
needs the same headers, siblings included. Therefore P2poolv2 shares
carry the bitcoin headers needed to connect its bitcoin prev to the
active bitcoin chain.

== Shares Carry Bitcoin Headers

A share carries the headers on the path from its bitcoin prev back to,
but not including, the point where that path meets its parent's
bitcoin prev:

* Most shares build on the same bitcoin block as their parent and
  carry nothing.
* The first share after a new bitcoin block carries one header.
* The first share after a bitcoin reorg carries the headers back to
  the fork point.

We call this list of bitcoin headers carried by a share as
`ShareHeaderBatch`. The first share in each `ShareHeaderBatch` also
carries the path back to the sender's active chain, because the
receiver may never have seen its parent. A syncing node learns
weeks-old orphans this way.

The headers travel beside the share header, outside the share header.
The share's proof of work already binds its bitcoin prev, and each
header's previous block hash fixes the header before it, so the share
already commits to every header it needs. A sender can only leave
headers out or add wrong ones. A missing header leaves the bitcoin
prev unknown, and the node waits for a copy with the headers, while a
wrong header fails the check.

=== Checking a Header

A node checks every carried header itself, with no RPC. A header
passes when:

* it links to a known header,
* its hash meets its `bits`,
* its `bits` is exactly the target the difficulty rules require, and
* its timestamp is at most two hours ahead of the local clock.

Within a 2,016-block period, the required `bits` equals the parent's.
At a retarget boundary, the node computes it with rust-bitcoin's
`CompactTarget::from_next_work_required`. On testnet4, the node also
applies the min-difficulty and BIP94 timewarp rules, which rust-bitcoin
leaves to the caller. The timestamp limit stops a fake branch from
setting timestamps that ease its `bits`.

A header that passes costs a full block of real bitcoin proof of work.
It does not prove that a valid body exists. The payout rule makes that
proof unnecessary, because it bounds how far off the paying chain paid
work can sit.

=== Accepting a Share

The same steps apply to a share header in a `ShareHeaderBatch` and to
an unsolicited share block. None of them waits on bitcoind.

. If the share's parent or an uncle is unknown, drop the share and ask
  the sender for headers, as the node does today for a missing parent.
. Check the carried headers, oldest first, and stop at the first
  failure. Headers that pass go into the store.
. If the bitcoin prev is now known, store the share header, then fetch
  and validate the body, payout rule included.
. Otherwise drop the share without a verdict, and remember its hash in
  a bounded, expiring cache so its children are dropped without a
  refetch.

Requiring a known bitcoin prev keeps nodes in agreement. Otherwise an
attacker can mine a share on a real orphan, send it with headers to
some nodes and without them to others, and make those nodes rebuild
different coinbases later. A share on a made-up bitcoin prev never
gets a header, so no node accepts it. An honest share is almost never
dropped: its parent arrived first, so its carried headers link onto a
known bitcoin prev.

== Rejecting Work on Stale Bitcoin Blocks Fails

Rejecting shares whose bitcoin prev is off the local bitcoind's active
chain fails for four reasons:

Bitcoin nodes disagree for a while:: A new bitcoin block reaches some
bitcoind nodes before others. A node whose bitcoind has not seen the
block rejects shares that build on it, and nodes that have seen it
accept them. The sharechains on the two groups of nodes diverge.

Reorgs strand honest work:: When a bitcoin block loses a race, every
share mined on it becomes invalid, and the sharechain rolls back to
the last share on the winning block.

Late shares look like stale shares:: A share mined just before a new
bitcoin block arrived looks the same as a share mined on an old block
on purpose. Telling them apart needs a timing rule, and nodes have a
clock synchronization delta of 11 bitcoin blocks from the MTP rule.

Slack pays hostile forks:: Accepting shares within a few blocks of the
local tip pays a hostile fork's miners for those first blocks, while
they attack the main chain.

== Solution: Pay Only Work Near the Paying Chain

Nodes accept every share whose bitcoin prev they know, so all nodes
hold the same shares. The decision moves from accepting a share to
paying it: each coinbase pays only the window shares eligible for the
share that builds it.

=== The Payout Rule

When a node builds or checks the coinbase of share S, it computes the
PPLNS window anchored on S's parent as before. It then pays each
window share only if that share is eligible for S. An ineligible share
keeps its place in the window and earns nothing.

In the diagram below, Shares 1 to 4 form Share S's PPLNS window. A
branch leaves the paying chain at Block 101; it stands for either a
hostile fork or a two-block orphan race. Share 3, one block into the
branch, is paid. Share 4, two blocks in, is not.

Eligibility is measured from S's bitcoin prev, never from the checking
node's bitcoind tip. It depends on headers alone, so every node that
knows the same headers rebuilds the same coinbase. A template that
lags the sharechain by one block still pays the newest shares, since
their bitcoin prev sits one block off its paying chain.

The rule changes share validity. Every node rebuilds every coinbase
and compares it exactly (`validate_bitcoin_payout`), and any mismatch
makes the share invalid. A rule that only honest job builders applied
fails, because validators must then accept coinbases built either
way.

=== Uncles

* An ineligible uncle earns nothing, and its nephew earns no bonus for
  it. Honest nephews gain nothing by including fork shares.
* An eligible uncle under an ineligible nephew earns its uncle weight.
  The nephew's bonus goes unpaid.
* Uncles still use no window slots.

=== When Nothing in the Window Is Eligible

A job builder whose window holds nothing eligible stops the node. Its
miners extend a sharechain that an attacker controls.

A node checking a share with nothing eligible marks the share invalid
and keeps running. Anyone can make such a share for one share's proof
of work, by naming a main chain block more than 14 days old. No honest
share meets this case: its paying chain ends at its node's current
template, and the recent shares in its window sit on or beside that
chain.

=== What the Design Costs

* A validity change, which needs a coordinated upgrade at a fixed
  sharechain height.
* Hostile forks earn for about two block intervals of their hashrate
  each time a fork starts.
* Header-only work one block off the paying chain earns, at the cost
  of a full block of proof of work.
* Shares on the second block of a losing two-block orphan race earn
  nothing. Such races are rare.
* Payout variance rises during a fork war.
* Signet gets weak protection, since a signet header's proof of work
  is nearly free.

== Alternatives Considered

Accept shares within K blocks of the local tip:: Pays a hostile fork's
miners for its first K blocks. Nodes also disagree for good after a
bitcoin reorg.
Pay only clean shares from the job builder:: Every node rebuilds every
coinbase, so validators must accept coinbases built with the rule or
without it.
Payout rule with bitcoind's headers only:: Nodes disagree on coinbases
whenever some bitcoinds never saw an orphan. The only consistent form
stops paying orphans.
Chainwork and fork-depth validity rules:: A header-only block passes
them, they still pay a fork's later blocks, and they need the same
headers as the payout rule.
Pass carried headers to bitcoind with `submitheader`:: Costs an RPC
round trip per header, and lets attackers load bitcoind. The local
check suffices.
Check only that a header meets its own `bits`:: A fake branch then
uses easy `bits`, at a quarter of a block's work per header or less.
