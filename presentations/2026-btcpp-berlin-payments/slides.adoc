// 2. Custody is control (2.5 min)

== Custody Is Control

* A pool does not need to reject your template
* It just stops counting your shares
* The accounting is the lever
* A pool is a company in a jurisdiction, so that is where pressure lands

[.notes]
--

centrally controlled network… the accounting is the lever."

// 3. Today every pool is custodial (2.5 min)

== Every Pool Is Custodial Today

* The coinbase pays the pool
* The pool pays miners later, under FPPS or PPLNS
* Payout thresholds keep small balances in custody for weeks
* SV2 and DATUM decentralize templates, not payouts

[.notes]
--
The industry has decentralized template construction but not payouts.
With SV2 Job Declaration or DATUM, small miners are still paid from the
pool's custody. Source: the comparison page on docs.p2poolv2.org.

// 4. P2Pool did it (3 min)

== P2Pool Did It in 2011

* A chain of shares, each a weak bitcoin block
* Every share's coinbase pays the PPLNS window
* A share that meets the bitcoin target becomes a block
* Miners are paid on-chain, directly, with no operator

[.notes]
--
This is the only prior custody-free solution at pool scale.
--

// 5. But the coinbase doesn't scale (2 min)

== The Coinbase Does Not Scale

* *Firmware cap:* Bitmain firmware limits coinbase outputs
* *Blockspace:* 1,000 outputs take about 3.4% of a block
* *Dust:* tiny payouts cannot be spent alone

[.notes]
--
The firmware cap directly capped how many miners P2Pool could pay, and
so how far it could reduce variance. The blockspace figure is from the
Vienna 2026 deck.

// 5b. What P2Poolv2 is: the sharechain and its tooling.

== What Is P2Poolv2

* A sharechain of weak bitcoin blocks, one share every 10 seconds
* *Decentralized accounting:* every node agrees on who mined what
* *Decentralized templates:* every miner builds its own block

[.notes]
--
P2Poolv2 revives P2Pool's idea: the sharechain runs Nakamoto consensus
with a lower target than bitcoin, so every node reaches the same PPLNS
accounting and checks every share's payouts. No operator keeps the
books, and no operator picks the transactions.
--

== A Sharechain With Transactions

* UTXO and Script transactions, validated like bitcoin
* Think Litecoin: bitcoin's transaction model on another chain
* But each coinbase is minted when a miner finds a share
* One share, one coinbase output, owned by the miner's key

[.notes]
--
The sharechain has no block subsidy schedule of its own. Its only new
value is the one share unit each shareblock's coinbase pays the miner
who found it. That output is an ordinary UTXO: the miner can hold it,
split it, or sell it.
--

== Bitcoin Tooling Carries Over

[.landscape]
Script · Taproot · PSBT · Descriptors · BDK · Electrum API

* *Electrum API:* wallets and explorers query the sharechain
* *BDK:* a wallet with descriptors, PSBTs, and hardware signers

[.notes]
--
Status to be careful about on stage: the Electrum server is planned as
stage 4 of WALLET_API_PLAN.md in the node repository, and the BDK
wallet is not built yet. Stock Electrum clients also have no concept of
an output that expires with its root height, so wallets need to show
that.
--

== What Can You Build?

* Sell shares for bitcoin by atomic swap
* Marketplaces that price shares by time left in the window
* Wallets that hold shares next to bitcoin
* Multisig and timelocked shares for pools of small miners

[.notes]
--
Motivate the room: once shares are UTXOs with Script, the whole bitcoin
toolbox applies to them. The first use is getting small miners paid,
which is where the rest of the talk goes.

Transition: "And beyond the sharechain, the landscape has changed since
2011."

// 7. The requirements (2.5 min)

== What Paying Miners Requires

. *Unilateral exits*
. *Commitment at template time*
. *Deterministic, verifiable payouts*
. *Scalability*
. *Small miner support*

[.notes]

== The Txid Problem

[graphviz, txid-problem, svg]
....
digraph txid {
  bgcolor="#0B0B0C"
  nodesep=0.8
  ranksep=0.3

  // Each hash batch rolls the extranonce, so the txid changes.
  cb1 [label="Coinbase tx\nextranonce 0001"]
  cb2 [label="Coinbase tx\nextranonce 0002"]
  cb3 [label="Coinbase tx\nextranonce 0003"]
  t1 [label="txid 3f9a…", fillcolor="#F7931A", fontcolor="#0B0B0C",
      width=1.6]
  t2 [label="txid c417…", fillcolor="#F7931A", fontcolor="#0B0B0C",
      width=1.6]
  t3 [label="txid 08be…", fillcolor="#F7931A", fontcolor="#0B0B0C",
      width=1.6]

  cb1 -> cb2 -> cb3 [label="  roll extranonce", color="#FFFFFF66"]
  cb1 -> t1 [label="sha256d"]
  cb2 -> t2 [label="sha256d"]
  cb3 -> t3 [label="sha256d"]
  {rank=same; cb1; t1}
  {rank=same; cb2; t2}
  {rank=same; cb3; t3}
}
....

No stable outpoint exists until the block is found.

[.notes]
--
Miners roll the extranonce inside the coinbase on every hash batch, and
the txid is sha256d of the coinbase transaction. A protocol that needs a
pre-signed spend of the coinbase output has nothing to sign at template
time. Signing after the block is found makes the signer a gatekeeper who
can refuse, which breaks unilateral exits.

Lightning commitment transactions, Ark VTXO trees, and statechain backup
transactions are all pre-signed spends of a known outpoint. The failure
is structural, not a detail to work around.
--

// 9. Today's L2s can't sit under the coinbase (3 min)

== Today's L2s Cannot Sit Under the Coinbase

[cols="3,3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| | Pre-signed spend of the coinbase outpoint | Takes custody | Unilateral exit

| Lightning | [.yes]#✓# | [.no]#✗# | [.no]#✗#
| Ark, no covenants | [.yes]#✓# | [.no]#✗# | [.no]#✗#
| Statechains | [.yes]#✓# | [.no]#✗# | [.no]#✗#
| Ecash | [.no]#✗# | [.yes]#✓# | [.no]#✗#
| Federation | [.no]#✗# | [.yes]#✓# shared | [.no]#✗#
|===

[.notes]
--
Lightning needs a signed commitment transaction spending the funding
output by txid before the channel opens; the 100-block coinbase maturity
would delay the channel anyway. Ark without covenants needs a VTXO tree
pre-signed by the ASP and participants, rooted at the shared output. A
statechain needs a pre-signed backup transaction spending a known
outpoint. Ecash avoids the txid problem only by taking custody.
--

== The Federation Detour

* "Put the coinbase in a multisig instead of one custodian"
* A t-of-n key is still custody, shared across a group
* Miners still cannot exit without the federation signing
* The lever remains: pressure t signers instead of one operator
* *Sock puppets:* one adversary can quietly hold several seats

[.notes]
--
The coinbase must commit to some key at template time. If that key is a
multisig, the group holds miners' rewards until it signs a payout. The
honest version is ecash: Fedimint makes the federation explicit and
gives the best experience for tiny payouts, but it distributes custody
rather than removing it.
--

== Ecash: Three Ways In

[cols="2,3,2",options="header"]
|===
| Case | How it works | Trust

| Separate Mint and Buyer (NUT-14)
| A buyer pays in ecash locked to the same hash as the share HTLC
| Atomic swap, trust-minimized

| Mint is the buyer
| The mint buys shares and holds the reward stream
| Atomic in form, high trust

| Skip sharechain
| A PPLNS node or a federation credits earnings as ecash
| Back to trusted operators
|===

Which would you recommend for a home miner earning a few thousand sats a day?

[.notes]
--
Case 1 uses Cashu NUT-11 and NUT-14 locks. The miner redeems the tokens
and reveals the secret; the buyer learns it from the mint's spent-proof
witness and claims the share. The free option still applies.

Case 2: after learning the secret, the mint could simply refuse the
swap. The upside is simplicity: instant, private payouts priced by time
left in the window.

Case 3 is Hydrapool-style, the ecash version of the federation detour,
with the same sock puppet risk.

Framing: the sharechain holds trustless value; ecash is the private,
instant last mile.
--

// 10. Covenants fix it (2 min)

== Covenants Fix It

* *CTV (BIP 119):* commits to outputs, not inputs
* *APO:* signatures skip the prevout, so refunds can be signed early
* *CCV / MATT:* a coinpool with unilateral exit from any leaf

Meets all five requirements, with no interaction at template time.
It needs a soft fork.

[.notes]
--
A coinbase output carrying a CTV hash commits non-interactively to a
payout tree; an Ark round tree could be committed the same way. With
APO, a channel refund can be signed before the coinbase txid exists.

Ask the room: which proposal fits the payout tree best? What does a leaf
exit cost at depth 10 against depth 20?
--

== Our Answer Today

* Pay the top N miners directly in the coinbase

== The Atomic Swap

[plantuml, atomic-swap, svg]
....
@startuml
<style>
root {
  FontSize 18
}
</style>
hide footbox
participant "Alice\n(miner)" as A
participant "Sharechain" as S
participant "Lightning / Ark" as L
participant "Bob\n(market maker)" as B

B -> A : Bob's public key
A -> A : pick preimage R\nH = SHA256(R)
A -> L : invoice or VHTLC for H
A -> S : lock share UTXO in HTLC(H, Bob's key)
B -> S : verify the HTLC
B -> L : verify the invoice
alt happy path
  B -> L : pay the invoice, locked to H
  L -> A : incoming payment locked to H
  A -> L : claim the payment by revealing R
  L --> B : R, as proof of payment
  B -> S : claim the share with R
else Bob never pays
  A -> S : reclaim the share after the CSV delay
end
@enduml
....

== Open Problems: L2 Builders

* *Timelocks:* the sharechain CSV must exceed the L2 expiry plus confirmation, inside the window
* *The free option:* Bob can wait before deciding to pay
* *Receiving:* which rail makes a few thousand sats painless to receive?

[.notes]
--
These timelock parameters are not specified yet.

Share value moves two ways: slow drift, which is near zero over an hour,
and block-find jumps. The chance of a block per hour is about h·T/600s,
where h is the pool's share of network hashrate: well under 1% at about
0.5 EH/s, and about 6% at 1% of the network.
--

== Closing the Free Option

. *Price it:* an upfront premium (Liu 2018; Han, Lin & Yu 2019)
. *Forfeitable deposit:* escrowed premium (Xue & Herlihy 2021)
. *Shrink the window:* settle the bitcoin leg over Lightning or Ark
. *Attribution rule:* the seller keeps block payouts until the claim confirms
. *Bitcoin-verified transfers:* no hashlock at all

[.notes]
--
Fix 2 also covers Nadahalli, Khabbazian & Wattenhofer, "Grief-free
Atomic Swaps" (2022); it works in Bitcoin script today. For fix 3, the
10s sharechain does not help alone: the bitcoin leg sets the option's
duration.
--

== Bitcoin-Verified Share Transfers

. The seller locks the share to a buyer, price, expiry, and depth _k_
. The buyer pays the price on bitcoin
. The buyer claims the share with a merkle proof
. Sharechain nodes check the payment against bitcoin headers

No secret to withhold, and the seller need not be online.

[.notes]
--
P2Poolv2 nodes already track bitcoin headers, so the sharechain can
verify SPV proofs directly. The payment goes to a per-offer destination
derived from the seller's key, so one payment cannot satisfy two offers.
The seller's refund is valid only after expiry plus k plus a grace
period, with no qualifying payment. The buyer still holds a short
option, limited by an expiry one to three blocks out.

Costs: bitcoin reorgs deeper than k; nodes that have not seen the block
must defer, not reject, the claim; the sharechain needs a way to verify
merkle proofs; and every trade costs one bitcoin transaction. Prior art:
BTC Relay.
--
